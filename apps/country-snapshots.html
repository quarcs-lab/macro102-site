Country Snapshots

Interactive macroeconomic data for every country, from the Penn World Table 11.0

Twelve interactive charts of the long-run macroeconomic data of 185 countries, with the Python code to rebuild each one in Google Colab.

The data cover 185 countries from 1950 to 2023. Hover over a chart to read its values, compare up to five countries, change the benchmark and the years, and open the Python code under any chart to rebuild it in Google Colab.

The page is inspired by Charles I. Jones’s Country Snapshots, the one-page summaries of every country that accompany his textbook Macroeconomics. Panels 1–6 follow his snapshots; panels 7–12 are new.

      Notes

      Reading the charts.

      • Each title gives the latest value of each series in the years shown, in parentheses, as in Jones’s snapshots. When several countries are compared, the latest values are in the legend.
      • With one country, the first series of a chart is the solid blue line and the second is the dashed line. In panels 5 and 9, the second series has its own axis on the right. When several countries are compared, each chart shows one series (choose it above the chart), with one line per country.
      • Benchmark. GDP per capita, GDP per worker and TFP are shown relative to the benchmark in the same year (benchmark = 100). Human capital is shown as a percent of the benchmark’s latest value, so its line still shows the country’s own progress. Jones uses the United States as the benchmark (and its 2019 value for human capital). Choose Levels to see the values themselves. A country that is its own benchmark is shown in levels.
      • World average. The world average is population-weighted, over a fixed sample of countries with complete data for 1970–2023, so that the average does not jump when countries enter the data. The samples have 158 (GDP per capita), 113 (GDP per worker), 129 (human capital) and 81 (TFP) countries.
      • Growth rate. Panel 2 shows the percent change of GDP per capita from the year before. The average in its title (the dashed line) is the average annual growth rate over the years shown, 100 × ln(ylast/yfirst) / (number of years), as in Jones’s snapshots.
      • The ratio (log) scale turns a constant growth rate into a straight line. GDP starts on the ratio scale, as in Jones’s snapshots. Switch it on or off for GDP, the exchange rate and population.
      • Missing years are gaps in the lines. Small countries and the years before 1970 have the least data.

      The variables. Every number is computed from the Penn World Table 11.0. These are the columns of the data file that the Python code reads (and that Download CSV gives):

      Column Definition (PWT 11.0 variables) Unit
      pop population, pop millions
      gdp_pc GDP per capita, rgdpo / pop 2017 US dollars (PPP)
      gdp_pw GDP per worker, rgdpo / emp 2017 US dollars (PPP)
      growth growth rate of GDP per capita, percent change of gdp_pc from the year before % per year
      inv investment share of GDP, 100 x csh_i % of GDP (current PPP)
      gov government consumption share of GDP, 100 x csh_g % of GDP (current PPP)
      hc human capital index, hc index
      nx net exports including the residual, 100 x (csh_x + csh_m + csh_r) % of GDP (current PPP)
      open openness, exports plus imports, 100 x (csh_x - csh_m) % of GDP (current PPP)
      xr exchange rate, xr national currency per US dollar
      tfp TFP level at current PPPs, ctfp USA = 1
      tfp_index TFP at constant national prices, rtfpna 2017 = 1
      ky capital-output ratio, cn / cgdpo ratio (current PPP)
      emp_pop employment-population ratio, 100 x emp / pop % of population
      hours average annual hours worked per worker, avh hours
      labsh labor share, 100 x labsh % of GDP
      price price level of GDP, pl_gdpo US GDP price level in 2017 = 1

      Rebuild a chart in Python. Open Show the Python code under any chart. The code reflects what the page shows (the countries, the benchmark and the years), uses only pandas and Plotly Express, and reads the data file from the book’s GitHub repository, so it runs in any empty Colab notebook. Change the choices in Step 2 and run the cell again. The data file is country_snapshots.csv, described in its README.

      Differences from Jones’s snapshots. These charts use PWT 11.0 (data through 2023); Jones’s latest version (January 2023) uses PWT 10.01 (data through 2019). PWT revises its past data with each release, so the values for the same year differ slightly between the two.
