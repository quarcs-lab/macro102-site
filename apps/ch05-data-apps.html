5.4 Does the model fit the data?

Fig. 3: Explaining capital across countries

Move the slider to change the depreciation rate; a rate of about 7 to 9 percent makes the model line run through the middle of the cloud.

Sources. Data: Penn World Table 11.0, Groningen Growth and Development Centre (DOI 10.34894/FABVLR), variables csh_i (investment share) and rgdpo (real GDP); cite as Feenstra, Inklaar and Timmer (2015), “The Next Generation of the Penn World Table”, American Economic Review 105(10). Capital is our own perpetual-inventory estimate from PWT investment, with 5 percent depreciation. The straight line is the Solow model’s prediction, \(K/Y = s/d\). Prepared file: data/ch05/fig5-3.csv (see the data notes). Chart: inspired by Fig. 5.3 in Jones (2024), Macroeconomics, 6th ed.
