Bolivia in the Data

Every data chart of the book, with Bolivia

This page is for readers in Bolivia, and for anyone who wants to study the Bolivian economy with the book’s charts. Bolivia is in the data of almost every chart, but it is never drawn by default, so the charts look the same for every reader. Three ways to see it:

  • Follow a link below. It opens the chart with Bolivia already drawn (the address ends in ?show=BOL).
  • Click “Bolivia” in a chart’s legend, or pick it in the chart’s menu.
  • In Colab, Bolivia is in the same data files: choose it in the widgets, or filter the data on "Bolivia" or "BOL".

The Snapshots

  • PWT Snapshots: Bolivia and its neighbors: the long run, 1950-2023 (Penn World Table 11.0).
  • WEO Snapshots: Bolivia and its neighbors: the short run and policy, 1980-2031 with the IMF’s projections. The WEO has no output gap or structural balance for Bolivia; the page fills them with the book’s estimates, marked as such.

What the labels mean

Label Where Bolivia’s data come from
Official (23 of 196 charts) INE or the Banco Central de Bolivia
International (96 of 196 charts) the World Bank, the IMF, the ILO, the WHO, the Penn World Table, Maddison, WID, OWID or the External Wealth of Nations
Proxy (28 of 196 charts) a related series stands in for the chart’s concept
Book estimate (18 of 196 charts) computed by the book from official data (method in the notes)
Not available (31 of 196 charts) no Bolivian series exists for this chart

Book estimates. Bolivia publishes no output gap or potential output. The book estimates them with a Hodrick-Prescott filter of real GDP (INE, and the IMF’s growth projections to 2031 at the end of the annual series), and adjusts the government balance for the cycle with them. HP estimates are least reliable in the last few years.

Exchange rate. The charts use the official rate of the Banco Central de Bolivia (6.86 to 6.96 bolivianos per dollar from 2011, about 12 after mid-2026). From 2023 dollars also traded in a parallel market at a much weaker rate, but no reliable monthly series of that rate is published, so the book does not draw one.

All Bolivian series, with their sources, units and years: data/bolivia/.
